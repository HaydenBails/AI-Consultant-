# Upwork Proposals — AI Operations Audit

Two templates for inbound-style bidding on Upwork. The strategy is the same in
both: **position the $400 audit as the smart, low-risk first step** before anyone
commits to a build — it de-risks the client's spend and gives you a paid entry
point that leads to the $1,500 implementation.

Placeholders: `{{FOUNDER_NAME}}`, `{{SAMPLE_REPORT_LINK}}`, `{{LANDING_PAGE_URL}}`,
`{{CALENDAR_LINK}}`.

Honesty rules apply: I'm newly launched, no prior-client claims, no invented
results. The fictional-but-labeled sample report is the proof of work quality.
On Upwork, be upfront that the profile is new — clients see the "new freelancer"
badge anyway, so owning it reads as confident, not weak.

**First-line hook = the most important part.** Upwork clients skim. Open by
quoting or paraphrasing something specific from *their* post so it's obvious the
proposal isn't copy-pasted. Recipe (under 2 min): pull one concrete phrase from
the job post — the tool they named, the task, or the outcome they want — and
mirror it back in line one.

## Template A — for "automation expert wanted" posts

*(Client already knows they want automation and may name tools like Zapier, Make,
n8n, or a specific workflow.)*

**First-line hook `[[quote their goal]]`** — e.g. "You want to stop re-entering
candidates from Indeed into Bullhorn by hand — that's a clean automation, and
worth mapping before building."

> Hi [Client name],
>
> [[quote their goal]]
>
> I build exactly this kind of workflow automation (n8n / Make / Zapier + API
> glue). Before we build anything, I'd suggest starting with my AI Operations
> Audit: for $400 I map your full process, then send a report in 48 hours showing

>

> guessing, and the report is yours to keep either way. Most audits lead straight
> into a fixed-price build (typically $1,500 for the top recommendation), so the
> $400 comes off as a smart first step, not extra overhead.

> labeled): {{SAMPLE_REPORT_LINK}}
>
> Fair disclosure: I recently launched this service, so my Upwork profile is new —
> the sample shows the quality and the guarantee keeps it risk-free on your side.

## Upwork tactical notes
- Keep the first two lines tight — that's all the client sees in the proposal
  preview. Lead with the hook, not with "Hi, my name is."
- If the post has screening questions, answer them briefly *above* the template.
- Don't bid the audit as the job price if the posted budget is a build budget —
  instead, propose the audit as step 1 and the build as step 2 in the call.
- Never claim past Upwork jobs, ratings, or clients you don't have.
