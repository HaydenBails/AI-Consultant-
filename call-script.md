# 20-Minute Discovery Call — Script & Outline

The goal of this call is **not** to sell a build. It's to (a) confirm the agency
has real administrative drag, (b) show you understand their world, and (c) get a
$400 audit purchased. The audit does the selling of the $1,500 build later.

Honesty stance for the whole call: you're newly launched, you say so plainly if
asked, and you lean on the guarantee and the (clearly-labeled, fictional) sample
report as proof of quality — never on clients or results you don't have.

---

## Time budget (20 minutes)
- **0–2 min** — Frame the call
- **2–12 min** — Diagnostic questions (listen ~70% of the time)
- **12–16 min** — Present the $400 audit
- **16–20 min** — Handle objections + next step

---

## 0–2 min — Frame

> "Thanks for the 20 minutes. Here's the plan: I'll ask a handful of questions
> about how your agency actually runs day to day, and if I can see where you're
> losing time to admin, I'll tell you exactly how my audit would map it. If I
> can't, I'll tell you that too. Sound fair?"

Sets a low-pressure, diagnostic tone and signals you won't hard-pitch.

---

## 2–12 min — Five diagnostic questions

Ask, then shut up and take notes. These mirror the intake questionnaire, so a
"yes, let's do the audit" flows straight into the form.

1. **"Walk me through what happens from the moment a new role comes in to the
   moment a candidate is submitted — who touches it and where does it slow down?"**
   *(Surfaces the core workflow and the manual handoffs.)*

2. **"Where does your team re-type or copy the same information between systems —
   job boards, your ATS, spreadsheets, submittal docs?"**
   *(Double-entry is the highest-value automation target.)*

3. **"How many roles are you typically working at once, and roughly how many
   candidates move through per week?"**
   *(Volume — this is what sizes the hours/dollars saved. No volume, no ROI.)*

4. **"What has to get chased manually — candidate follow-ups, interview
   scheduling, client updates, timesheets?"**
   *(Reminder/nudge work automates well and owners feel this pain acutely.)*

5. **"If you got 5–10 hours a week back, what would you or your recruiters
   actually do with it?"**
   *(Ties saved time to their real goal — more calls, more roles, no new hire —
   which you'll echo when presenting.)*

**Listen for the number moment:** as soon as they describe volume + a repeated
manual task, do the rough math out loud ("so that's ~X times a week someone's
hand-keying that") — it previews the audit's value without over-claiming.

---

## 12–16 min — Present the $400 audit

Keep it short and structured:

> "Here's what I'd do. For $400, you and I run through a 15-question intake — takes
> you about 15 minutes. Within 48 hours I send back a branded report that shows:
> the specific tasks worth automating, ranked; how many hours and dollars each one
> saves *based on your numbers*; a 90-day roadmap; and what to leave alone. If it
> doesn't find at least 10 hours a month worth recovering, you don't pay.
>
> You keep the report either way. If you want me to build the top recommendation,
> that's a separate fixed-price project — usually around $1,500 — but there's zero
> obligation. The audit stands on its own."

Then: **"Want me to send the intake link and get you scheduled for the 48-hour
turnaround?"** — that's the close. Stop talking.

---

## 16–20 min — Objection responses

### "Why should I trust someone new?"
> "Fair — I launched this recently and I'm not going to pretend I've got a wall of
> logos. Two things make this safe for you: first, the guarantee — if the audit
> doesn't find at least 10 hours a month worth recovering, you pay nothing, so the
> downside is capped at zero. Second, the sample report I sent shows exactly the
> depth and format you'd get — that's a fictional demo agency, clearly labeled, but
> the analysis is the real thing. Judge me on the work, not the tenure."

### "Can't ChatGPT do this?"
> "For brainstorming, sure — ChatGPT will happily list generic automation ideas.
> What it won't do is sit inside *your* workflow, use *your* volume and *your*
> hourly costs to rank what's actually worth it, and tell you what to leave alone.
> The value isn't the idea list; it's the prioritized, costed plan grounded in your
> numbers — and then someone who can actually build it. That's the gap between a
> chatbot answer and a decision you can act on."

### "Just quote me the build."
> "I could throw a number out, but I'd be guessing — and you'd be paying me to
> guess. The audit is how we make sure the first build is the *right* one, so your
> $1,500 goes at the highest-value task instead of the first one we happened to
> talk about. It also credits toward the direction, not against it: worst case you
> spend $400 and get a full plan; best case we find something worth way more than
> the build costs. If you're set on moving fast, we can run the audit this week and
> scope the build on the very next call."
>
> *(If they still insist on a flat quote and clearly have budget + a defined task:
> don't lose the deal over process — offer to scope a fixed price on a follow-up
> once you've seen the workflow, but hold the line that you won't quote blind.)*

---

## After the call
- **Booked:** send the intake link immediately while it's warm; confirm the
  48-hour clock starts when the intake comes back.
- **Not now:** ask permission to check back in ~30 days; log it in the sheet.
- **No fit (no volume / no drag):** say so honestly and don't push the audit — a
  clean "you probably don't need this yet" builds the reputation the roster can't
  yet.
